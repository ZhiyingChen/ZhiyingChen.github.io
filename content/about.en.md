## From mathematics to operations and systems

I studied English Language and Literature with a mathematics minor at Nanjing University, then Operational Research with Data Science at Edinburgh. I wanted to understand how mathematics could help people make choices about real problems.

As an algorithm engineer at Youhualin, I worked on manufacturing planning, scheduling, and supply chain projects. Delivery dates, material availability, capacity, and changeovers had to become variables, constraints, and objectives. My focus was on understanding those rules and building models that could solve them.

At Air Products, my work moved closer to daily logistics and operations: the information customer-service teams needed, how to plan visits, and how to allocate transport resources. I began paying more attention to preparing data, presenting results, and how people actually used the tools.

In manufacturing operations systems, I now work across planning data, version management, shortage analysis, and delivery coordination. Algorithms still matter, alongside questions such as whether a result can be explained, a manual change can be traced, and a system can support work that happens every week.

That is the thread connecting my studies in mathematics and operations research to optimization engineering, operational digitalization, and manufacturing systems.
